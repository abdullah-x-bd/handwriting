The Human Development Index, or HDI, is an index used to measure the overall level of human development in a country. It was introduced by the United Nations Development Programme. It gives a broader picture of development than income alone.

HDI is based on three main dimensions of human life.

The first is health, which is measured through life expectancy at birth. A higher life expectancy generally indicates better health conditions.

The second is education. It considers the level of schooling received by adults and the expected years of schooling for children.

The third is standard of living, which is measured through income per person.

Countries are ranked according to their HDI performance. A higher HDI indicates better achievements in health, education and living standards.

The importance of HDI is that development is not treated only as an increase in national income. A country may have high income but still perform poorly in education or health. Therefore, HDI provides a more complete measure of people's well being and quality of life.
