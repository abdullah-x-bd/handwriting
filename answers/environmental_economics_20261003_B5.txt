Environmental economics is the branch of economics that studies the relationship between economic activities and the natural environment. It examines how production and consumption affect natural resources, pollution and environmental quality. It also studies how environmental problems can be controlled through economic policies.

The main scope of environmental economics includes:

1. Natural resource use: It studies the efficient use of resources such as forests, water, minerals, land and energy.

2. Pollution: It examines the economic causes and effects of air, water, soil and other forms of pollution.

3. Externalities: Environmental damage caused by producers or consumers may affect other people without compensation. Environmental economics studies such external costs.

4. Valuation of environmental goods: It tries to estimate the economic value of clean air, forests, biodiversity and other environmental resources.

5. Environmental policy: It studies instruments such as pollution taxes, subsidies, regulations and tradable permits.

6. Sustainable development: It examines how economic development can continue without seriously damaging natural resources.

7. Climate change: It studies the economic causes, costs and possible solutions to global warming.

Thus, environmental economics helps governments and society make better decisions by including environmental costs and benefits in economic planning.
