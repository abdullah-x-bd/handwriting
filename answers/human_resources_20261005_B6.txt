Human Resource Development, commonly called HRD, refers to the process of improving the knowledge, skills, abilities, health and productive capacity of people. It aims to develop human beings so that they can contribute effectively to economic and social development.

The major features of Human Resource Development are as follows.

First, HRD is a continuous process. Human development does not stop after formal education. People need to continue learning and improving their skills throughout their working life. Changes in technology and production methods make continuous training necessary.

Second, education is an important part of HRD. School education, higher education, technical education and vocational education help individuals acquire knowledge and skills. Education also improves their ability to understand and use new technology.

Third, HRD includes training and skill development. Training improves the ability of workers to perform particular jobs. Skill development is especially important in a modern economy where industries require specialised workers.

Fourth, health is a major element of HRD. A healthy worker can work more efficiently and regularly. Public health, nutrition, sanitation and access to medical services therefore contribute directly to human resource development.

Fifth, HRD increases productivity. Better educated, trained and healthy workers are generally more productive. Higher labour productivity can increase output, income and economic growth.

Sixth, HRD develops both individuals and organisations. At the individual level, it improves knowledge, confidence and employment opportunities. At the organisational level, it improves efficiency, performance and the ability to adopt new methods.

Seventh, HRD supports technological development. New technology can be useful only when workers have the knowledge required to operate and improve it. Human resource development therefore helps an economy adopt modern technology.

Eighth, HRD helps in better utilisation of manpower. It identifies the abilities of individuals and helps place them in suitable occupations. This reduces the wastage of human resources.

Ninth, HRD has a long term nature. Investment in education, health and training may take time to produce results, but its benefits can continue for many years.

Finally, HRD contributes to social development. Education and employment opportunities can reduce poverty, inequality and social exclusion. They can also improve people's participation in economic and social life.

Therefore, Human Resource Development is much wider than simple job training. It includes education, health, skills, knowledge and the overall development of human capabilities. It is an important foundation for both individual welfare and national economic development.
