Part A.3

According to A. C. Pigou, economic welfare is closely related to the size and distribution of the national dividend, which broadly refers to the real national income available to society. Pigou gave two important conditions under which economic welfare can be increased.

The first condition is an increase in the size of the national dividend. Other things remaining the same, if the total quantity of goods and services available to society increases, economic welfare is likely to rise. A larger national dividend allows a greater satisfaction of economic wants.

The second condition concerns the distribution of the national dividend. Pigou argued that a transfer of income from richer persons to poorer persons generally increases economic welfare, provided that such redistribution does not significantly reduce total national income. This argument is based on the principle of diminishing marginal utility of income. An additional rupee provides greater utility to a poor person than to a rich person.

Thus, Pigou associated maximum economic welfare with both a larger national dividend and a more equal distribution of income. However, the two objectives may sometimes conflict if redistribution adversely affects production or incentives.
