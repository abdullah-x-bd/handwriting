The United Nations Conference on the Human Environment was held in Stockholm, Sweden, from 5 to 16 June 1972. It was the first major international conference devoted specifically to environmental problems at the global level. The conference was important because it placed environmental protection firmly on the international agenda.

The conference developed a broad framework for international environmental cooperation. Its two major outcomes were the Stockholm Declaration and the Action Plan for the Human Environment.

The Stockholm Declaration contained 26 principles. It recognised that human beings have a right to live in an environment of adequate quality and also have a responsibility to protect and improve the environment for present and future generations.

One important principle was that natural resources such as air, water, land, forests and wildlife should be protected through careful planning and management. The conference recognised that many natural resources are limited and should not be used without considering future needs.

Another important principle concerned the responsibility of states. Countries have the sovereign right to use their own natural resources, but they should ensure that activities within their territory do not cause environmental damage to other countries or areas beyond national jurisdiction.

The conference also recognised the close relationship between environment and development. Developing countries argued that poverty and lack of development were themselves important environmental problems. Therefore, environmental protection should not prevent economic development.

The conference emphasised the need for financial and technological assistance to developing countries so that they could improve environmental protection while continuing their economic development.

The Action Plan contained 109 recommendations for international environmental action. These recommendations covered environmental assessment, environmental management and international cooperation.

The major recommendations of the Stockholm Conference included:

1. Governments should take measures to control air, water and soil pollution.

2. Natural resources such as forests, water, land and wildlife should be used carefully and conserved.

3. Endangered species and important natural ecosystems should be protected.

4. Environmental considerations should become a part of economic and development planning.

5. Countries should cooperate in dealing with environmental problems that cross national boundaries.

6. Scientific research and environmental monitoring should be strengthened.

7. Countries should exchange environmental information, knowledge and technology.

8. Environmental education and public awareness should be promoted.

9. Developing countries should receive financial and technical support for environmental protection.

10. Governments should improve planning of human settlements so that urban development does not create excessive environmental damage.

A major institutional outcome of the conference was the creation of the United Nations Environment Programme, commonly known as UNEP. UNEP became an important international body for coordinating environmental activities within the United Nations system.

The Stockholm Conference also encouraged many countries to create environmental ministries, agencies and laws. Environmental concerns gradually became a more important part of national policy and international cooperation.

However, the conference also showed differences between developed and developing countries. Developed countries were more concerned with pollution and industrial environmental damage, while many developing countries were concerned about poverty, economic growth and access to resources. The conference tried to bring these concerns together.

Therefore, the Stockholm Conference of 1972 was a landmark in the history of global environmental policy. It established basic principles of environmental responsibility, encouraged international cooperation and created an institutional foundation for later global environmental agreements and discussions on sustainable development.
