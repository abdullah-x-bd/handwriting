Human Resource Development refers to the systematic development of the knowledge, skills, health, abilities and productive capacity of people. It aims to improve the quality of human beings so that they can participate more effectively in economic and social activities.

Human resources are different from physical resources because people can learn, develop skills, become more productive and adapt to changing conditions. Therefore, expenditure on education, training, healthcare and skill formation is considered an important investment in human development.

The main elements of Human Resource Development include education, vocational training, technical education, health, nutrition, employment related skills and continuous learning. Education provides general knowledge and intellectual ability. Training provides specific skills required for particular occupations. Health and nutrition improve the physical and mental capacity of workers.

HRD is important because modern economic development depends heavily on skilled and knowledgeable people. Machines, technology and capital cannot be used efficiently without trained human beings. Better human resources increase labour productivity, improve innovation and make it easier for an economy to adopt new technology.

Human Resource Development is closely related to Manpower Planning.

Manpower Planning is the process of estimating the present and future requirement of workers in different sectors and occupations. It examines how many workers are required, what type of skills are needed and whether the available labour force can meet these requirements.

The relationship between HRD and Manpower Planning can be understood in several ways.

First, Manpower Planning identifies the quantity and type of labour required, while HRD helps develop the quality and capability of that labour. For example, if manpower planning shows that the economy will need more engineers, nurses or technicians in the future, the education and training system can be expanded accordingly.

Second, Manpower Planning helps HRD programmes become more relevant. Without proper planning, educational institutions may produce large numbers of graduates in areas where employment opportunities are limited while industries face shortages in other skills.

Third, HRD provides the trained workers required to achieve manpower plans. Planning alone cannot solve a shortage of skilled workers. Schools, universities, training centres and healthcare systems are required to actually develop the necessary human resources.

Fourth, both are connected with economic development planning. When the government plans the expansion of agriculture, industry, infrastructure, healthcare or information technology, it must also estimate the manpower required for these sectors. Human Resource Development then prepares people for these opportunities.

Fifth, they help reduce the problem of skill mismatch. Skill mismatch occurs when the skills possessed by workers are different from the skills required by employers. Proper manpower forecasting combined with education and training can reduce this problem.

Sixth, HRD and Manpower Planning are important in periods of technological change. New technologies may reduce demand for some occupations while increasing demand for others. Planning helps identify these changes, while HRD allows workers to learn new skills.

There are, however, some difficulties in manpower planning. Future labour demand is difficult to predict because technology, economic growth and industrial structure can change unexpectedly. There may also be delays between identifying a skill shortage and training enough people to solve it. Therefore, manpower plans should remain flexible and should be regularly revised.

Human Resource Development has a wider meaning than Manpower Planning. Manpower Planning mainly concentrates on the labour requirements of the economy or an organisation. HRD is concerned with the overall development of people's capabilities and welfare.

Thus, the two concepts are closely connected. Manpower Planning identifies future human resource requirements, while Human Resource Development creates and improves the people needed to meet those requirements. Together they contribute to better employment, higher productivity and long term economic development.
