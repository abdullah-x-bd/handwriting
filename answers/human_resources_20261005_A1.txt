[HEADING] A.1

Human Resource Planning means estimating the present and future need for workers in an organisation or in the economy and making arrangements to meet that need. Its main purpose is to ensure that the right number of people, with the right skills, are available at the right place and at the right time.

Human Resource Planning begins with an assessment of the existing workforce. It examines the number of employees, their qualifications, skills, experience and productivity. After this, future requirements are estimated according to expected growth, technological change, retirement, expansion and other factors.

It also includes planning for recruitment, training, promotion and replacement of workers. If there is a shortage of skilled workers, training and recruitment can be increased. If there is excess manpower, workers can be transferred or used in other productive activities.

Thus, Human Resource Planning helps in the proper use of labour, reduces shortages and unemployment, improves productivity and supports the long term development of an organisation as well as the economy.
