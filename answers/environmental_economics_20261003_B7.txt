Global warming refers to the long-term rise in the average temperature of the Earth. It is mainly caused by the increasing concentration of greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane and nitrous oxide in the atmosphere.

Human activities such as burning coal, oil and gas, deforestation, industrial production and transportation have increased greenhouse gas emissions.

The major environmental effects of global warming are:

1. Melting of glaciers and polar ice: Rising temperatures cause glaciers and ice sheets to melt.

2. Sea-level rise: Melting ice and the expansion of warmer seawater increase sea levels, threatening coastal areas.

3. Changes in rainfall: Some regions may experience heavier rainfall and floods, while others may face droughts.

4. Extreme weather events: Heat waves, intense rainfall and some forms of extreme weather may become more frequent or severe.

5. Loss of biodiversity: Many plants and animals may find it difficult to survive when temperatures and habitats change rapidly.

6. Effects on agriculture: Changes in temperature and rainfall can reduce crop productivity in many areas.

7. Water scarcity: Changing rainfall and glacier loss can affect freshwater availability.

8. Ocean warming: Higher ocean temperatures can damage marine ecosystems, including coral reefs.

Thus, global warming is not only an increase in temperature. It can disturb ecosystems, water resources, agriculture and human settlements. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions and protecting natural ecosystems are therefore important for limiting its effects.
