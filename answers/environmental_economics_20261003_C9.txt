Environmental degradation means the deterioration of the natural environment because of pollution, overuse of resources, deforestation, loss of biodiversity and other harmful human activities. It has become a major concern because rapid industrialisation, urbanisation, population growth and rising consumption have increased pressure on nature.

One major problem is the depletion of natural resources. Forests are being cleared, groundwater is being extracted at a high rate, fertile land is becoming degraded and many natural habitats are being destroyed. If these resources are used faster than they can recover, future generations may face serious shortages.

Pollution is another important concern. Air pollution is caused by industries, vehicles, construction activities and the burning of fossil fuels. Water pollution results from untreated sewage, industrial waste and agricultural chemicals. Soil pollution, plastic waste and improper disposal of solid waste also damage ecosystems. Pollution reduces the quality of life and increases health expenditure.

Climate change has made these problems more serious. The concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere has increased because of human activities. As a result, average global temperatures are rising. This contributes to heat waves, changing rainfall patterns, droughts, floods, glacier melting and sea-level rise.

Environmental degradation also causes loss of biodiversity. Destruction of forests, wetlands and other ecosystems threatens many plant and animal species. This also reduces important ecosystem services such as pollination, water purification, soil fertility and climate regulation.

These environmental problems have major implications for future development. Agriculture may suffer because of irregular rainfall, water shortages, higher temperatures and land degradation. This can affect food production and rural incomes.

Industries and cities may also face increasing water and energy shortages. Coastal areas may become more vulnerable to flooding and sea-level rise. Governments may have to spend more on health care, disaster management, environmental restoration and climate adaptation.

Environmental degradation can also increase inequality. Poorer households usually have fewer resources to protect themselves from pollution, floods, droughts and other environmental risks. Many of them also depend directly on forests, land and water for their livelihoods.

Future development must therefore be based on sustainable use of natural resources. Governments should encourage renewable energy, energy efficiency, sustainable agriculture, forest conservation, better public transport, waste management and pollution control.

Economic planning should also consider environmental costs. Development projects should not be judged only by their immediate economic benefits, but also by their long-term effects on natural resources and human welfare.

Thus, environmental protection is necessary for long-term development. Economic growth that destroys the natural resource base cannot remain sustainable. The main challenge is to achieve development while maintaining a healthy environment for present and future generations.
