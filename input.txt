Shah Waliullah was one of the most important Islamic scholars and reformers of eighteenth-century India. He was born in Delhi in 1703 into a respected religious family. His father, Shah Abdur Rahim, was a well-known scholar and was associated with the compilation of the Fatawa-e-Alamgiri during the reign of Mughal emperor Aurangzeb. Shah Waliullah received his early education from his father and later became a teacher and religious scholar.

At a time when the Mughal Empire was declining and Indian society was facing political instability, Shah Waliullah believed that Muslims needed religious, social, and intellectual reform. He argued that Muslims should return to the basic teachings of the Quran and Sunnah and avoid practices that had no clear basis in Islam. At the same time, he tried to reduce disagreements between different schools of Islamic thought and encouraged greater unity among Muslims.

One of his major contributions was translating the Quran into Persian. Persian was widely understood by educated people in India at the time, while many people could not understand Arabic. His translation therefore made the teachings of the Quran more accessible.

Shah Waliullah also wrote extensively on religion, society, economics, and politics. His most famous work, Hujjat Allah al-Baligha, discussed the wisdom behind Islamic laws and explained how religion could contribute to a just and balanced society. He was concerned about economic inequality, excessive taxation, and the weakening of political authority.

He also took an interest in the political problems of his time and corresponded with several rulers and leaders. Shah Waliullah died in Delhi in 1762. His writings and teachings had a lasting influence on Islamic scholarship in South Asia, and many later Muslim reform movements drew inspiration from his ideas.
